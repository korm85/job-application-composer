# Email Outreach Templates

## When to use
Email is the longer-format version of a DM. Use when:
- Applying directly to a hiring manager or recruiter via email (not through an ATS form)
- Following up on a LinkedIn DM that has progressed to email
- Sending a cover letter as an email body rather than an attachment
- Responding to a recruiter who has emailed you first

Email allows 3--4 paragraphs where a DM allows 2--3 sentences. Use the extra space for a second proof point, a constraint that shows technical depth, or a clear "why this company" close. Do not pad it.

---

## Structural pattern (extracted from Medison, Oriient, and Windward cover letters)

All three cover letters follow the same four-move structure:

**Move 1: State the role and land the relevance immediately.**
No opener fluff. Name the role. Then immediately map their core product problem to something you have already shipped. (Medison: "The core of what you're looking for... is what I've done twice." Oriient: "[Company] turns [X] data into [Y]. At Oqton, I built the same thing.")

**Move 2: Prove the match with a specific product and result.**
Name the product (AMVero, Simulation Suite). Name a number if confirmed (5 enterprise customers, 5 months, 90% accuracy, 98% reduction). Do not list every credential -- use the one or two that are most directly relevant to their problem.

**Move 3: Surface a constraint or depth signal (optional, for technical roles).**
The Medison letter uses this: "shipped under ITAR and SOC 2 -- role-based access, auditability, and data residency were part of the spec from the start." The Windward letter skips this and acknowledges a domain gap instead ("I haven't worked in maritime before"). Use the constraint paragraph when the role requires technical rigor in compliance, data architecture, or AI reliability. Skip it when the fit is more about product instinct and domain transfer.

**Move 4: Close with a brief "why this company" and a forward-facing statement.**
One or two sentences only. Medison: "Building a platform that helps get treatments to patients who wouldn't otherwise have access is meaningful work." Oriient: "That's what I've done at Oqton and it's what I want to keep doing." Windward: closes without a why-company sentence, relying on the domain match to carry it.

Pattern: **role + immediate relevance, proof with specifics, optional depth signal, brief mission close.**

## Template A: Standard cover letter email (0-to-1 / enterprise product)

*Use when applying for roles that require taking a product from concept to market or managing a complex B2B platform. Adapted from the Medison cover letter structure.*

```
Hi [Name / Hiring Team],

I'm applying for the [Role Title] role at [Company].

The core of what you're looking for -- [1-sentence description of the job requirement in their terms] -- is what I've done twice. Most recently at Oqton, I built [AMVero / an AI-based detection product] from zero: defined the scope, ran discovery across engineers, operators, and shop floor managers, coordinated UX, data, and engineering into one release, and signed five enterprise customers in five months. Before that, I took a third-party physics engine and built three workflow modules on top of it for medical, aerospace, and defense, each requiring its own MVP boundaries and validation.

[Optional paragraph for technical depth: Both products had to handle complex data with real constraints. The detection product drives autonomous decisions on live production runs, and it shipped under ITAR and SOC 2: role-based access, auditability, and data residency were part of the spec from the start, not added at the end. I also built an internal knowledge platform on a locally hosted LLM connected to live product documentation, which replaced a constant flow of technical questions to engineering with a tool the teams actually used daily.]

I know how to translate what a business stakeholder describes in a meeting into something an engineering team can build and a customer will actually use. That has been the job across every product I've owned.

[1--2 sentence close: why this company or mission is meaningful. Keep it specific to them, not generic.]
```

```
I haven't worked in [target domain] before, but the core problems feel familiar and I pick up new domains quickly.
```

## Rules for all email templates

- Subject line: "Application for [Role Title] -- Michael Korenevsky" (for applications) or "Following up -- [Role Title] conversation" (for follow-ups).
- No em dashes: use commas or colons.
- Contact line uses ` • ` separator, not `|`.
- LinkedIn URL: short form only, no http:// prefix.
- Do not include metrics not confirmed in facts.md.
- Do not echo JD language verbatim unless it genuinely matches your experience.
- Do not use: "leveraged", "spearheaded", "synergistic", "cutting-edge", "actionable insights", "strategic alignment", "developer velocity."
- "Senior" does not appear in the email body, only in the role title if the role title includes it.
