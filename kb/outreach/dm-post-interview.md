# Post-Interview Follow-Up DM

## When to use
After any interview stage: recruiter screen, hiring manager call, panel interview, or take-home review. Send within 24 hours while the conversation is fresh. Use the same channel the interview was conducted through or initiated from (LinkedIn DM if that is how you connected, email if the process has been email-based).

## Calibrated ask
The ask at this stage is not a next step -- it is a signal of continued interest and a courtesy close. Do not ask "when will I hear back?" Do not push for a timeline unless you have a competing offer. State that you are looking forward to next steps and leave the door open. If you are waiting on a timeline you legitimately need, it is acceptable to note that in one sentence.

## Template

```
Hi [Name],

Thanks for the conversation [today / yesterday]. [One sentence: what you got from it -- genuine, specific, not flattering.]

[1--2 sentences: reinforce the single strongest connection between what was discussed and your background. Reference something specific from the conversation -- not a generic re-statement of your experience.]

[1-sentence close: confirm your interest and invite next steps without pressure.]

[Name]
```

---

## Structural pattern (from cover letters)

The cover letters reveal how Michael closes: the Medison letter closes with "Building a platform that helps get treatments to patients who wouldn't otherwise have access is meaningful work. That's the kind of product I want to be building." The post-interview DM follows the same logic but anchors it to something from the actual conversation rather than the company's stated mission. The Oriient letter closes with "That's what I've done at Oqton and it's what I want to keep doing." -- forward-facing, brief, no ask.

Pattern: **thank + one specific takeaway from the conversation + reinforce one credential + close with interest, no ask.**

## Worked examples

### Example 1: After recruiter screen

```
Hi [Name],

Thanks for the call today. Learning more about how the [Team] is structured and the stage the product is at was useful.

Our conversation about [specific topic: e.g. the on-premise deployment requirements / how discovery works with enterprise customers] maps directly to what I managed at Oqton: [1-sentence anchor, e.g. "AMVero shipped with SOC 2 and ITAR as hard constraints, not afterthoughts" or "I ran customer discovery across operators, engineers, and shop floor managers before any spec was written"].

Looking forward to next steps.

Michael Korenevsky
linkedin.com/in/michael-korenevsky
```

### Example 2: After hiring manager interview

```
Hi [Name],

Thank you for the time today. The conversation about [specific topic from interview: e.g. how the team handles noisy sensor data / the challenge of building confidence in AI-generated recommendations] was exactly the kind of problem I find interesting.

It reminded me of curating training data for AMVero's computer vision model: model alignment directly determined whether a live, high-value production run was stopped or continued. Getting that threshold right mattered.

I'm genuinely excited about the role. Happy to answer any follow-up questions from my end.

Michael Korenevsky
linkedin.com/in/michael-korenevsky
```

### Example 3: After panel / late-stage interview

```
Hi [Name],

Thank you for today. The panel discussion gave me a much clearer picture of how [Company]'s [product / platform / squad] is evolving and where the gaps are.

[Specific observation from the discussion: e.g. "The point about needing someone who can own both the customer conversation and the technical spec resonated. At Oqton I was the PM writing the requirements and sitting in the customer review -- no hand-off."]

I'm very interested in the role and confident I can contribute from day one. Looking forward to hearing how the process moves forward.

Michael Korenevsky
linkedin.com/in/michael-korenevsky
```
