# Warm Outreach DM

## When to use
Weak tie, prior meeting, or mutual connection. Use when you have met the person before (even briefly), have a mutual connection you can name, are reaching out to a former colleague, or are connecting via a shared community (university, industry event, former employer).

## Calibrated ask
At this depth you can ask for either a brief inside perspective on the team or role, or whether they would be comfortable passing your name to the hiring team. Do not lead with "can you refer me?" -- lead with the connection, then make the ask as an option not an obligation. "No pressure at all" signals you are not transactional.

## Template

```
Hi [Name],

[1-sentence connection anchor: how you know them, the mutual connection you share, or the context where you've crossed paths.]

[1--2 sentences: what you are exploring and why it connects to your background. Name the product or company. Keep it specific.]

[1-sentence ask: either a perspective on the team/role, or whether they would be comfortable sharing your profile. Explicitly take the pressure off.]

[Name]
```

---

## Structural pattern (from cover letters)

The cover letters do not model warm outreach directly, but the Oriient letter shows the closest pattern: it opens by naming the company mission explicitly ("Oriient turns indoor positioning data into operational intelligence"), then mirrors it against the sender's work ("At Oqton, I built the same thing"), then states the ask in product terms ("owning that full arc again"). In a warm DM the mirror is replaced by the relationship anchor, but the product-to-product matching logic is the same.

Pattern: **name the connection, match their context to your product, ask with an exit ramp.**

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Mutual connection

```
Hi [Name],

I hope you're doing well. I saw we both know [Mutual Name] from [context: Oqton / BGU / shared industry].

I'm reaching out because I saw an opening for [Role Title] at [Company] and the product problem maps closely to work I've done at Oqton: [1-sentence match, e.g. "building an AI platform from zero to enterprise customers" or "managing spatial data feeds across multiple machine types"].

Would you be open to sharing a quick inside perspective on the team, or if you'd be comfortable passing my name along? No pressure either way.

Michael Korenevsky
linkedin.com/in/michael-korenevsky
```

### Example 2: Reactivating a former colleague

```
Hi [Name],

It's been a while. Hope things are going well at [their company].

I'm currently exploring new roles and saw a [Role Title] opening at [Company]. Given your time there, I wanted to hear how you're finding the team and whether it's the kind of environment where someone with [0-to-1 AI product experience / enterprise SaaS background] would fit.

If you think it's a good match, I'd appreciate you passing my profile along, but mostly just wanted to hear your take first.

Michael
```

### Example 3: Same company, different team (internal warm reach)

```
Hi [Name],

I came across your profile while researching [Company]'s [product area]. I'm applying for the [Role Title] on the [Team] and wanted to get a ground-level view before the interview process.

My background is in [AI-powered enterprise SaaS / manufacturing software]. At Oqton I owned [AMVero / the Simulation Suite] end-to-end. The [specific aspect of their product] caught my attention because [specific connection to your experience].

Would you be open to a 10-minute call or even a quick message exchange about how the team operates? No rush.

Michael Korenevsky
linkedin.com/in/michael-korenevsky
```
